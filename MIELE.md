## Strategic Themes
- João’s durable value at Miele is increasingly centered on systems architecture, forecast/process design, automation, explainability, and AI-enabled operating models rather than classic reporting-heavy FP&A. His real value is in systems architecture, not just FP&A reporting, and he is trying to transform Miele from a patchwork of manual, inherited, partially conflicting finance/commercial processes into a coherent business-steering system.
- A recurring Miele pattern is structural entanglement: planning, forecasting, accounting, controlling, pricing, treasury/currency, working capital, and legal-entity complexity are formally separate but tightly coupled in real execution.

- João’s early instinct on arrival appears repeatedly validated: technically overbuilt solutions aimed at non-technical business users create adoption friction, weak ownership, and workarounds. He strongly prefers simple usable tools over fancy unusable ones.
- Internal AI value is increasingly framed as the combination of model capability + company data + workflow context + adoption design, not as standalone access to a generic model.
- A recurring AI/enablement principle: safe approved paths must be easier than shadow workarounds, or employees will route around governance.
- Reusability, explainability, and role clarity are emerging as three core design principles across João’s Miele work.
- João keeps attacking the gap between management reporting and true decision support, especially in RDC/country/channel allocation logic, bonus processing, and demand-planning integration.
- A large share of Miele complexity comes from weak translation layers between systems, structures, and users, not from the core business logic itself.

## Forecasting & Planning Architecture
- Forecast quality should not stop at topline numbers; it needs explicit assumptions, phasing, confidence, risk/opportunity framing, and margin logic.
- The working target architecture is reusable channel models that share a common output structure so they consolidate cleanly to a total-Nordics view.
- KRT Forecast 1 is emerging as the first presentation-grade/reference channel model.
- KRT means Kitchen Retail customers and is owned by Wei Zhou in João’s FP&A team.
- KRT Forecast 1 logic is converging toward:
  - start from net sales
  - break down by customer and/or mix logic
  - connect list price, discount %, bonus, and SBC
  - derive margin / product-margin view with better explainability
- Product-margin explainability is a next maturity gap, not yet fully solved.
- D2C Forecast 1 is the active reusable template direction for other channels.
- D2C/business-ownership principle: business should own the commercial forecast inputs; finance/controlling should own translation into SAC/system-ready forecast structures.
- Projects/professional business likely needs a lighter country/assumption-based model rather than major-customer granularity.
- A monthly upload format for SAC/forecasting is currently ambiguous/wrong for the actual forecasting need; ownership and phasing/loading logic need clarification before standardizing country files.

## Process Pain Points
- Month-end pressure remains linked to close acceleration, accrual discipline, and late PR/invoice handling that distort downstream quality.
- João and team want earlier visibility during the month and fewer last-minute surprises at close.
- There is a live push to move soft close materially earlier, toward Workday 2, which raises practical dependency questions around accruals, PR timing, and invoice discipline.
- Monthly German GAAP reporting via SAP remains a meaningful process-change backdrop, not just a technical reporting tweak.
- Bonus/CRM workflow remains a hardening candidate:
  - market/business side owns accrual correctness
  - ESF/system experts support technical execution
  - agreement setup and accrual correctness must be fixed before close, not only at settlement
- Bonus/commission/reconciliation work remains too manual, underdocumented, and too dependent on key people; João repeatedly pushes for standardization, cross-training, and stable monthly controls instead of year-end heroics.
- A broad Miele process pattern keeps repeating:
  - broken or unclear ownership across teams/systems
  - reactive escalation instead of proactive monitoring
  - too much manual retrieval/reconciliation work
  - fragmented end-to-end design across sales, service, back office, and finance
- João repeatedly challenges recurring finance/reporting mechanics that consume effort without improving commercial decisions, especially where statutory/tax allocation logic spills into management steering.

## Channel & Commercial Model Signals
- Premium strategy should not rely on one blunt “top 20% of market volume” framing; category, household affordability, willingness-to-pay, and channel role need clearer definition.
- ERT / retail partner strategy needs clearer partner-role design rather than treating every partner the same.
- D2C growth should come from total growth and sharper role definition, not from simply cannibalizing partner volume.
- Kitchen retail strategy needs stronger premium-partner selection and preferred-partner positioning.
- Experience centers should evolve from passive showrooms into true premium sales/experience environments.
- D2C / experience-center staff should become more sales-capable while preserving premium experience quality.
- Registration, service data, installed-base knowledge, and customer identity/history should be treated as strategic commercial levers, not just aftersales admin.
- João sees demand planning, sales planning, and finance forecasting as one integrated workflow; when they are disconnected, Miele creates avoidable inventory, tariff, margin, and working-capital damage.
- João strongly prefers simpler usable planning/account-planning tools over technically fancy templates that commercial teams will not realistically adopt.
- Sweden remains a major underpenetrated opportunity in KRT and ERT, but unlocking it requires better partner-role design, sharper account focus, and more coherent commercial execution rather than generic effort.
- AI-related retail/commercial use cases with the strongest apparent relevance include better meeting/partner preparation, service/support knowledge assistance, store-photo or shelf-analysis workflows, and making product/content data more AI-search- and recommendation-friendly.
- Product data accessibility and quality are emerging as strategic prerequisites for future AI-enabled commercial execution, not just classic digital hygiene.
