## Main takeaways
### 1) Leadership is explicitly framing 2025 as a transformation year
- Martin describes 2025 as a year of major organizational and systems transformation:
  - IT changes
  - D2C changes
  - finance setup changes
  - HR/shared-service/analytics changes
- Despite this, leadership is proud that the region still protected profitability relatively well.
- One key message: transformation must not become an excuse for losing sight of customers, topline, and financial results.

### 2) Strategic clarity is increasing at group/regional level
- Martin stresses that Miele is now much clearer about where future growth is expected to come from:
  - kitchen
  - D2C
  - online revenue
  - service/supply chain excellence
  - portfolio/international growth
- At the same time, “maintain” categories and markets still matter.
- Important nuance: categories like SDA/laundry and stable markets like Denmark/Norway are still important, just not the main disproportionate growth engines.

### 3) Sweden is clearly the key Nordic growth focus
- Sweden is described as the strategic growth market in the Nordic cluster.
- Denmark/Norway are more established/stable markets.
- Finland receives less attention because of scale.
- This reinforces the larger cross-batch pattern that Sweden is the main commercial transformation battlefield.

### 4) The commercial dialogue is intentionally cross-functional
- Martin is explicit that this should not be a one-expert one-function discussion.
- Finance should have views on marketing.
- Service should have views on D2C.
- The intention is to force dialogue across functions, not let each team present in isolation.
- This is important context for João: finance participation in commercial strategy is explicitly legitimized here.

### 5) New GfK data becomes strategically important
- Team is newly using / trusting GfK market-share and category data more seriously.
- Data quality and coverage appear much better than in the past, including kitchen retail.
- This creates a more credible outside-in competitive lens than internal-only data.

### 6) Sweden market picture: strong growth, but Miele still structurally weak
- Sweden market growth is strong, around double digits in the data presented.
- Miele share is improving slightly, but still small versus major competitors.
- Electrolux is especially strong.
- Bosch/Siemens remain important premium/perception competitors.
- The team recognizes that consumer premium perception in Sweden is not sufficiently differentiated in Miele’s favor.

### 7) Product-margin concern is sharper than topline concern
- Finance discussion in the session shows:
  - net sales / topline start to 2026 is strong
  - but product margin is materially behind budget
- Laundry/entry-level mix is a major issue.
- João pushes hard on the seriousness of margin deviation and does not accept a relaxed interpretation.
- This is a very strong signal of João’s style: not satisfied with topline comfort if margin quality is structurally weak.

### 8) Forecast quality is treated as a strategic issue, not just a finance issue
- Discussion links poor prior forecasting to downstream budget distortion.
- Leadership explicitly says if forecast 2 / forecast 3 are wrong, next-year budget becomes unrealistic.
- This supports the recurring “forecast-one philosophy” emerging in other transcripts: assumptions and realism matter more than political optimism.

### 9) Service profitability turnaround is recognized
- Service is highlighted as moving dramatically from heavy negative profitability toward breakeven / near profitability.
- This is treated as a significant operational achievement.

### 10) Cost savings / NP program contribution was substantial
- Martin emphasizes that indirect spend and personnel cost savings materially protected market result.
- Without those savings, profitability would have been much worse.
- Important interpretation: 2025 profitability improvement was not only operational/commercial; it was also heavily supported by cost programs.
